# Homilies
## Luke 21:34-36 | Gospel of the day
> "But take heed to yourselves lest your hearts be weighed down with dissipation and drunkenness and cares of this life, and that day come upon you suddenly like a snare; for it will come upon all who dwell upon the face of the whole earth. But watch at all times, praying that you may have strength to escape all these things that will take place, and to stand before the Son of man."
### St Cyril of Jerusalem | Catechetical Lectures, 15.1-3 (adapted)
We preach not one coming of Christ only, but a second also, far more glorious than the first. The first was a sign of his patience; the second brings the crown of the kingdom. In the first he was wrapped in swaddling clothes in a manger; in the second he will be clothed in light.
So do not be weighed down by the cares of this life. We do not know the day, and it is not for us to know it. Keep watch, and prepare, so that the day may find us standing, not asleep.

## Mark 13:33-37 | Tomorrow, First Sunday of Advent
> "Take heed, watch; for you do not know when the time will come. It is like a man going on a journey, when he leaves home and puts his servants in charge, each with his work, and commands the doorkeeper to be on the watch. Watch therefore, for you do not know when the master of the house will come, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning, lest he come suddenly and find you asleep. And what I say to you I say to all: Watch."
### St Charles Borromeo | Pastoral Letters (adapted)
Beloved, this is the acceptable time that the Spirit calls the season of salvation, the time of the Lord's coming. The Church asks us to remember how great is the love of the Lord who came to us, and to prepare our hearts each year to receive him.
The Church does not only want us to recall that he came long ago. She wants us to be ready now: he who came once in the flesh is ready to come again, at any hour, into our hearts.

## Luke 1:26-38 | The Annunciation
> In the sixth month the angel Gabriel was sent from God to a city of Galilee named Nazareth, to a virgin betrothed to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary. And he came to her and said, "Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with you!" But she was greatly troubled at the saying, and considered in her mind what sort of greeting this might be. And the angel said to her, "Do not be afraid, Mary, for you have found favor with God. And behold, you will conceive in your womb and bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus." And Mary said, "Behold, I am the handmaid of the Lord; let it be to me according to your word." And the angel departed from her.
### St Bernard of Clairvaux | Homilies in Praise of the Virgin Mother, 4.8-9 (adapted)
You have heard, O Virgin, that you will conceive and bear a son. The angel awaits your answer, and we too are waiting, for the word of pity. The price of our salvation is offered to you; if you consent, we shall be set free at once.
Answer quickly, O Virgin. Speak the word the earth and heaven are waiting for. Open your heart to faith, your lips to consent, your womb to the Creator. Behold, the one desired of all nations is standing at the door and knocking.

## Luke 10:38-42 | Martha and Mary
> Now as they went on their way, he entered a village; and a woman named Martha received him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, who sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching. But Martha was distracted with much serving; and she went to him and said, "Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me." But the Lord answered her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her."
### St Augustine | Sermon 103 (adapted)
Martha's work was good: she served the Lord in his body. But Mary had chosen the better part. Martha was busy with many things; Mary was intent on one. The many will pass; the one remains.
What Mary chose grows greater, and what Martha did will one day cease, for in heaven no one will be hungry to be fed. So the Lord does not blame Martha's work. He tells her where her work is going.

## Matthew 6:6-8 | Praying in secret
> "But when you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you. And in praying do not heap up empty phrases as the Gentiles do; for they think that they will be heard for their many words. Do not be like them, for your Father knows what you need before you ask him."
### St Cyprian of Carthage | On the Lord's Prayer, 4 (adapted)
When we pray, let our words be ordered and quiet. Think that you stand in the sight of God. God hears the heart, not the voice. He who sees our thoughts does not need to be reminded by shouting.
Hannah, praying for a son, spoke in her heart; her lips moved but her voice was not heard, and God heard her. Pray like that: briefly, in secret, with faith.

# Saints
## St Cyril of Jerusalem | Catechetical Lectures, 15.1
We preach not one advent only of Christ, but a second also, far more glorious than the former.

## St Bernard of Clairvaux | Sermon 5 on Advent, 1 (adapted)
We know that there are three comings of the Lord. The third lies between the other two. In the first he came in the flesh and in weakness; in the middle coming he comes in spirit and in power; in the last he will come in glory and majesty.

## St Bernard of Clairvaux | Homilies on Missus est, 2.17 (adapted)
In dangers, in doubts, in difficulties, think of Mary, call upon Mary. Let her not leave your lips, let her not leave your heart.

## St Teresa of Avila | Bookmark (Nada te turbe)
Let nothing disturb you, let nothing frighten you. All things are passing; God never changes. Patience obtains all things. Whoever has God lacks nothing. God alone suffices.

## St Augustine | Confessions, X.27
Late have I loved you, O Beauty ever ancient, ever new, late have I loved you! You were within me, but I was outside, and it was there that I searched for you. You were with me, but I was not with you. You called, you shouted, and you broke through my deafness.

## St Thérèse of Lisieux | Story of a Soul, Manuscript C
For me, prayer is a surge of the heart; it is a simple look turned toward heaven, it is a cry of recognition and of love, embracing both trial and joy.

## St Francis de Sales | Introduction to the Devout Life, II.12 (adapted)
Remember to retire at various times into the solitude of your heart, even while you are outwardly busy with work or conversation. This mental solitude cannot be disturbed by the many people around you, for they are not standing about your heart but about your body.
