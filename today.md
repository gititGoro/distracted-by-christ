# Homilies
## Luke 21:29-33 | Gospel of the day
> And he told them a parable: "Look at the fig tree, and all the trees; as soon as they come out in leaf, you see for yourselves and know that the summer is already near. So also, when you see these things taking place, you know that the kingdom of God is near. Truly, I say to you, this generation will not pass away till all has taken place. Heaven and earth will pass away, but my words will not pass away."
### St Gregory the Great | Homilies on the Gospels, 1 (adapted)
The world is growing old. Like a body that in youth was strong and now bends under age, it is pressed by troubles, and its troubles are the signs our Lord foretold. He did not tell us these things to frighten us, but so that the end, when it comes, should find us ready.
So he points to the fig tree. When its leaves come, we know summer is near; when the world withers, we should know the kingdom is near. What passes is not worth our grip. Heaven and earth will pass away; hold instead to the word that does not.

## Mark 13:33-37 | Sunday, First Sunday of Advent
> "Take heed, watch; for you do not know when the time will come. It is like a man going on a journey, when he leaves home and puts his servants in charge, each with his work, and commands the doorkeeper to be on the watch. Watch therefore, for you do not know when the master of the house will come, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning, lest he come suddenly and find you asleep. And what I say to you I say to all: Watch."
### St Ephrem the Syrian | Commentary on the Diatessaron, 18 (adapted)
Christ kept the hour of his coming hidden so that we would keep watch, each of us thinking he might come in our own day. Had he told us the hour, his coming would mean little; we would wait idly until the date drew near.
He said he would come, but not when, and so every generation and every age longs for him. Watch, then: not only with open eyes, but with a heart that does not sleep.

## Luke 10:38-42 | Martha and Mary
> Now as they went on their way, he entered a village; and a woman named Martha received him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, who sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching. But Martha was distracted with much serving; and she went to him and said, "Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me." But the Lord answered her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her."
### St Augustine | Sermon 103 (adapted)
Martha's work was good: she served the Lord in his body. But Mary had chosen the better part. Martha was busy with many things; Mary was intent on one. The many will pass; the one remains.
What Mary chose grows greater, and what Martha did will one day cease, for in heaven no one will be hungry to be fed. So the Lord does not blame Martha's work. He tells her where her work is going.

## Matthew 6:6-8 | Praying in secret
> "But when you pray, go into your room and shut the door and pray to your Father who is in secret; and your Father who sees in secret will reward you. And in praying do not heap up empty phrases as the Gentiles do; for they think that they will be heard for their many words. Do not be like them, for your Father knows what you need before you ask him."
### St Cyprian of Carthage | On the Lord's Prayer, 4 (adapted)
When we pray, let our words be ordered and quiet. Think that you stand in the sight of God. God hears the heart, not the voice. He who sees our thoughts does not need to be reminded by shouting.
Hannah, praying for a son, spoke in her heart; her lips moved but her voice was not heard, and God heard her. Pray like that: briefly, in secret, with faith.

## John 15:4-5 | The vine and the branches
> "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me. I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in me, and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."
### St Cyril of Alexandria | Commentary on John, 10 (adapted)
The Lord calls himself the vine, and us the branches, to show how much we gain by being joined to him and how important it is to remain in his love. As branches draw their life from the vine, we draw ours from him.
Those joined to him by faith share his nature through the Spirit. Cut off, a branch withers, whatever it was doing before.

## Luke 18:13 | The tax collector's prayer
> But the tax collector, standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, "God, be merciful to me a sinner!"
### St Augustine | Sermon 115 (adapted)
He stood far off, yet he drew near to God. The Lord is high, yet he looks on the lowly. The Pharisee raised himself up and God stayed far from him; the tax collector stood far away and God came close.
He did not dare lift his eyes. He looked at himself so that God would look at him with mercy, and he went home justified.
