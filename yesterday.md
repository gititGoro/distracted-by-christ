# Homilies
## Luke 21:20-28 | Gospel of the day
> "But when you see Jerusalem surrounded by armies, then know that its desolation has come near. Then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains, and let those who are inside the city depart, and let not those who are out in the country enter it; for these are days of vengeance, to fulfil all that is written. Alas for those who are with child and for those who give suck in those days! For great distress shall be upon the earth and wrath upon this people; they will fall by the edge of the sword, and be led captive among all nations; and Jerusalem will be trodden down by the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles are fulfilled. And there will be signs in sun and moon and stars, and upon the earth distress of nations in perplexity at the roaring of the sea and the waves, men fainting with fear and with foreboding of what is coming on the world; for the powers of the heavens will be shaken. And then they will see the Son of man coming in a cloud with power and great glory. Now when these things begin to take place, look up and raise your heads, because your redemption is drawing near."
### St Augustine | Letter 199, to Hesychius, On the End of the World (adapted)
In this passage the Lord speaks of two things at once: the fall of Jerusalem, which came in its own generation, and his coming at the end, which no one can date. Luke keeps them apart more clearly than the others. The armies around the city belong to the first; the Son of Man on the clouds belongs to the second.
So we should not reckon the years and fix a day. The Lord comes daily to his Church, and he will come at the last. Whether the end is near or far, the end of each one's life is near, and as each is found on his last day, so he will be found on the last day of the world.

## Psalm 96:11-13 | He comes to judge the earth
> Let the heavens be glad, and let the earth rejoice; let the sea roar, and all that fills it; let the field exult, and everything in it! Then shall all the trees of the wood sing for joy before the Lord, for he comes, for he comes to judge the earth. He will judge the world with righteousness, and the peoples with his truth.
### St Augustine | Exposition of Psalm 95(96), 14-15 (adapted)
He came the first time, and he will come again. He came quietly, to be judged; he will come openly, to judge. Why should the trees of the wood rejoice? Because he comes. Let us not be afraid of his coming; let us love it, and amend our lives while there is time.
If you love him now, you will not fear him then. He who is to be our judge is now our advocate. Do not wait to be afraid; begin to love.

## Mark 13:33-37 | Sunday, First Sunday of Advent
> "Take heed, watch; for you do not know when the time will come. It is like a man going on a journey, when he leaves home and puts his servants in charge, each with his work, and commands the doorkeeper to be on the watch. Watch therefore, for you do not know when the master of the house will come, in the evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning, lest he come suddenly and find you asleep. And what I say to you I say to all: Watch."
### St Ephrem the Syrian | Commentary on the Diatessaron, 18 (adapted)
Christ kept the hour of his coming hidden so that we would keep watch, each of us thinking he might come in our own day. Had he told us the hour, his coming would mean little; we would wait idly until the date drew near.
He said he would come, but not when, and so every generation and every age longs for him. Watch, then: not only with open eyes, but with a heart that does not sleep.

## Luke 10:38-42 | Martha and Mary
> Now as they went on their way, he entered a village; and a woman named Martha received him into her house. And she had a sister called Mary, who sat at the Lord's feet and listened to his teaching. But Martha was distracted with much serving; and she went to him and said, "Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone? Tell her then to help me." But the Lord answered her, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things; one thing is needful. Mary has chosen the good portion, which shall not be taken away from her."
### St Augustine | Sermon 103 (adapted)
Martha's work was good: she served the Lord in his body. But Mary had chosen the better part. Martha was busy with many things; Mary was intent on one. The many will pass; the one remains.
What Mary chose grows greater, and what Martha did will one day cease, for in heaven no one will be hungry to be fed. So the Lord does not blame Martha's work. He tells her where her work is going.

## John 15:4-5 | The vine and the branches
> "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you, unless you abide in me. I am the vine, you are the branches. He who abides in me, and I in him, he it is that bears much fruit, for apart from me you can do nothing."
### St Cyril of Alexandria | Commentary on John, 10 (adapted)
The Lord calls himself the vine, and us the branches, to show how much we gain by being joined to him and how important it is to remain in his love. As branches draw their life from the vine, we draw ours from him.
Those joined to him by faith share his nature through the Spirit. Cut off, a branch withers, whatever it was doing before.

## Luke 18:13 | The tax collector's prayer
> But the tax collector, standing far off, would not even lift up his eyes to heaven, but beat his breast, saying, "God, be merciful to me a sinner!"
### St Augustine | Sermon 115 (adapted)
He stood far off, yet he drew near to God. The Lord is high, yet he looks on the lowly. The Pharisee raised himself up and God stayed far from him; the tax collector stood far away and God came close.
He did not dare lift his eyes. He looked at himself so that God would look at him with mercy, and he went home justified.

# Saints
## St Thomas Aquinas | O Sacrum Convivium, antiphon for Corpus Christi
O sacred banquet, in which Christ is received, the memory of his Passion is renewed, the mind is filled with grace, and a pledge of future glory is given to us.

## St Ignatius of Antioch | Letter to the Ephesians, 20
Breaking one bread, which is the medicine of immortality, the antidote that we should not die, but live for ever in Jesus Christ.

## St Augustine | Sermon 272 (adapted)
If you are the body of Christ and his members, it is your own mystery that is placed on the Lord's table. Be what you see, and receive what you are.

## St Francis of Assisi | Letter to the Entire Order (adapted)
Let the whole of mankind tremble, the whole world shake and the heavens exult, when Christ, the Son of the living God, is present on the altar in the hands of a priest.

## St Teresa of Avila | Bookmark (Nada te turbe)
Let nothing disturb you, let nothing frighten you. All things are passing; God never changes. Patience obtains all things. Whoever has God lacks nothing. God alone suffices.

## St Augustine | Confessions, I.1
You have made us for yourself, O Lord, and our heart is restless until it rests in you.

## St John Vianney | Catechism on Prayer
My little children, your hearts are small, but prayer stretches them and makes them capable of loving God.
